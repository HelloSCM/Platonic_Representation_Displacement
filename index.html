TL;DR

Independently trained models agree more on the directions between representations than on the representations themselves. Across 44 vision and language encoders, this shared geometry points to a practical way to transfer capabilities: cache frozen model features once, then train lightweight modules.

The central idea

Different coordinates.
Shared directions.

Two maps can place landmarks differently and still give the same directions. We ask the same question of neural representations: does moving from one input to another trace a similar direction in different models?

02 / Inside the representation

Concepts give the geometry
its structure.

Within a model, parent concepts are approximately orthogonal to the variations that distinguish their children. Across models, concept displacements are strongly aligned. These observations motivate an account of how sample-level geometry emerges.

01

Keep the parent, add the distinction.

A child such as “dog” retains the shared “mammal” component. Its distinguishing increment lies approximately orthogonal to the parent.

vdog = vmammal + Δdog

02

Separate siblings.

Child increments anti-align within the variation subspace, helping distinguish siblings such as “dog” and “cat.”

03

Carry structure into samples.

If samples combine concept components, shared concept directions can explain their noisier sample-level counterparts.

Explore shared semantics, private components, and model depth+

A label-free cross-model consensus decomposition ranks directions by agreement across jointly aligned models. High-consensus directions retain more linearly decodable semantic information. Linear and RBF dependence measurements suggest nonlinear relationships between shared and low-consensus components.

Where does shared geometry live?

Vision–vision agreement follows corresponding layers; language–language agreement spans more layers. Cross-modal agreement concentrates in deep vision layers.

03 / A test through training

Change the objective.
Change what stays aligned.

Training trajectories distinguish pointwise agreement from displacement agreement. Feature matching raises both; relational supervision can improve directions while leaving pointwise similarity nearly unchanged.

Ten-epoch experiments described in Section 4.5; the paper also reports analogous trends from random initialization.

04 / From geometry to capability transfer

Cache once.
Cast a stronger model.

Shadow Casting reuses frozen model representations to train lightweight transfer modules. Our proof of concept, ShadowCLIP, combines CLIP with DINO’s visual features and relational supervision from an LLM.

01

Extract the shadows.

Run the training set through frozen CLIP, DINO, and the language teacher once. Cache their representations for reuse.

02

Connect complementary strengths.

A CLIP-gated SwiGLU residual incorporates DINO features. A text-side MLP learns semantic relations from LLM2Vec-Gen-Qwen3-4B.

03

Train the small modules.

Use image–text contrastive learning and relational distillation on CC12M. Zero-initialized gates preserve CLIP’s initial behavior.

At inference

The LLM teacher is removed. The vision branch still uses both CLIP and DINO for new images.
