We show that what models share is not the location of samples in representation space, but the directions (displacement vectors) between them. Under orthogonal alignment, these vectors are substantially preserved across independently trained vision and language encoders.

Two maps of the same city need not agree on coordinates to give you similar directions. The displacement vectors between landmarks are preserved, even when their absolute positions on the page are not.

Neural networks enjoy an analogous freedom: each learns its own coordinate system. After alignment, does the displacement between two samples in one model point in the same direction as the displacement in another?

Geometry

Within a model, parent concepts are approximately orthogonal to their child variation vectors; across models, concept displacements are parallel.

Experiments

We evaluate 44 pretrained models: 25 vision transformers spanning three sizes and six pretraining paradigms, plus 19 language models. Cross-model comparisons use rigid alignment only.

Parallelism

Concept-level parallelism exceeds sample-level parallelism. Under rigid alignment alone, displacement vectors between concepts are preserved across independently trained models, while their pointwise positions differ. Cross-Model similarity increases with model size and is near zero for randomly initialized networks.

All panels report cosine similarity. PCA, whitening, and Procrustes alignment are fitted only on the alignment split and kept fixed during evaluation on disjoint inputs.

Orthogonality

Parent concepts remain approximately orthogonal to their child variation vectors across layers. If samples are weighted combinations of concepts, we hypothesize that within-model orthogonality and cross-model concept parallelism explain sample-level parallelism. Degrading either concept-level condition weakens sample-level parallelism in controlled simulations.

Modality

The pattern of Cross-Model similarity differs systematically by modality. Vision-model agreement concentrates at corresponding layers; language-model agreement extends broadly across middle and deep layers. Nearly every language-model layer aligns with deep vision-model layers. This cross-modal pattern is consistent with shared semantic structure.

Dynamics

Pointwise and Cross-Model similarity are independently controllable by training objective. Fine-tuning leaves Pointwise nearly unchanged while reducing Cross-Model; feature distillation raises both; relational distillation raises only Cross-Model. The 100-epoch experiments reproduce the same pattern from random initialization.

Decomposition

The shared component aligns linearly across models but remains nonlinearly entangled with the private component. At matched dimensions, it has higher Cross-Model similarity and retains more linearly decodable semantic content. Linear dependence between the components is low; RBF dependence is substantially higher.

ShadowCLIP

ShadowCLIP instantiates Shadow Casting: capability transfer via a cached forward pass. A frozen CLIP backbone is augmented by two lightweight residual modules, using DINO representations and relational distillation from an LLM teacher.

Training

Cached Representations

Freeze the CLIP, DINO, and LLM backbones and cache their representations once over the training set.

Vision and Language Residuals

A SwiGLU residual uses CLIP as a gating signal and DINO as a value; a two-layer MLP augments CLIP text features.

Training Objective

Train on CC12M with image–text contrastive loss and relational distillation from an LLM teacher. Zero-initialized gates make ShadowCLIP exactly CLIP at initialization.

Inference

At inference, the LLM teacher is no longer needed, while the vision branch still uses DINO to encode unseen images. ShadowCLIP retains the CLIP text encoder and its trained residual for inference efficiency.

Compared with full fine-tuning of CLIP-Large, ShadowCLIP requires 95% fewer trainable parameters (20M vs. 400M). Caching reduces training-phase FLOPs by a factor of 2,000; including the one-time precomputation, total FLOPs over three training epochs are 9× lower.

Experimental Results

We evaluate on zero-shot classification (11 benchmarks), image–text retrieval (MSCOCO, Flickr30k), and visual-centric understanding (MMVP).
